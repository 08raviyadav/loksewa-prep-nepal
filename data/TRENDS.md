# Google Trends / What Students Search — Lok Sewa Prep Nepal

**Research date:** 2026-10-25 / 2026-10-26 (snapshot — see §5 Limits)
**Method:** `browser.search` (10 queries) + attempted text fetch of Google Trends.
No live-browser session was used. No Google Trends volume, index (0–100),
region, or time-series number is quoted anywhere in this file, because none
could be verified (see §5).

> **How to read the ranking below:** this is a *demand proxy*, not a Trends
> chart. Rank is by converging evidence: (a) how often a term/topic appears
> across independent prep sites' FAQs, guides and home pages; (b) whether
> competitors lead with it; (c) whether past-paper analysis calls it
> "most asked" / "repeated"; (d) whether a current vacancy / syllabus change
> (2082/83) is driving searches now. Where evidence is thin, that is stated.

## 2. Common doubts / questions students ask

Short answers are drawn from the sources in §1. **Verify against
psc.gov.np / tsc.gov.np / the vacancy notice before publishing any of
these as site copy** — several sources conflict (flagged ⚠️).

1. **Is there negative marking? How much?**
   Yes — 20% per wrong answer on PSC prelims. On a 2-mark question that is
   −0.4; on a 1-mark question, −0.2. Blank answers lose nothing.
   ⚠️ One NaSu source quotes "0.25 marks deducted" per wrong answer on a
   100×1 paper — that matches a *different* (older / 100-question) paper
   format, not the revised 50×2 prelim. Do not quote 0.25 without the
   format it belongs to.
   Sources: https://www.edusoftacademy.com/blog/kharidar-syllabus-2083-84 ·
   https://lokshewa.online/blog/nayab-subba-exam-preparation.html

2. **What are the pass marks? Has it changed?**
   First Paper / prelim pass is **45 out of 100** for Kharidar, NaSu and
   Section Officer (raised from 40 from FY 2081/82). Main-paper pass is
   40 per paper; TSC prelim pass is 50; NRB Assistant prelim pass is 40.
   ⚠️ Section Officer main pass is quoted as both 40 and 45 in different
   sources — verify per vacancy.
   Sources: https://merokalam.com/blog/lok-sewa-aayog-preparation-guide/ ·
   https://edusanjal.com/exam/assistant-second-class-nrb/

3. **Do prelim marks count in the final merit list?**
   No — not any more. Since FY 2081/82 the First Paper is purely
   qualifying: its marks are not added to the main-exam total. The old
   "50% of first-paper marks added" rule was removed. Many old books
   still print the old rule.
   Source: https://merokalam.com/blog/lok-sewa-aayog-preparation-guide/

4. **What is the age limit?**
   Generally **18–35** for open competition; **up to 40 for women and
   persons with disabilities**; health-service posts up to 45; permanent
   government employees are generally exempt from the upper cap. The
   exact cut-off date changes with each advertisement — always check the
   vacancy notice.
   Sources: https://www.kumarijob.com/blog/lok-sewa/how-to-pass-loksewa-exam ·
   https://loksewatayariapp.com/service/public-service-commission

5. **How many stages are there?**
   Usually 3: (1) preliminary / pre-qualifying MCQ, (2) main written
   exam (subjective papers), (3) final stage — computer skill test
   (10 marks, 15 min) + interview (30 marks; TSC interview 25).
   Section Officer adds a skill test + group test. Some posts add a
   practical.
   Sources: https://www.kumarijob.com/blog/lok-sewa/how-to-pass-loksewa-exam ·
   https://loksewatayariapp.com/exam/Kharidar

6. **Kharidar vs Nayab Subba — which is harder, and can a Kharidar apply for NaSu?**
   NaSu is harder: broader syllabus, deeper/analytical questions, and
   experienced Kharidars compete via promotion. Serving Kharidars can
   apply for NaSu through internal promotion after the required service
   period (typically 5 years).
   Source: https://lokshewa.online/blog/nayab-subba-exam-preparation.html

7. **How long does preparation take? Can I pass by self-study?**
   Typically **6–12 months** of consistent preparation (NaSu guides say
   6–9 months: 3–4 months syllabus + 2 months revision/MCQ + 1–2 months
   mocks, 5–6 hrs/day). Yes, self-study works — many pass without
   coaching — but coaching helps with structure/discipline. One full
   timed mock per week is the most repeated single piece of advice.
   Sources: https://www.kumarijob.com/blog/lok-sewa/how-to-pass-loksewa-exam ·
   https://lokshewa.online/blog/nayab-subba-exam-preparation.html

8. **Which books / resources are best?**
   Most-cited: Goodwill and Pairavi guide books, GK by Bal Krishna
   Pokhrel, daily newspapers (Gorkhapatra), plus official syllabus +
   past papers from psc.gov.np. TSC advice: buy *one* standard textbook
   (B.Ed./M.Ed.) + *one* syllabus-based guide — not 5–6 guides.
   Sources: https://www.kumarijob.com/blog/lok-sewa/how-to-pass-loksewa-exam ·
   https://www.collegenp.com/blog/becoming-a-government-teacher-process-prep-and-success-strategy

9. **Where do I apply / what documents do I need?**
   Federal PSC online portal **psconline1.psc.gov.np** (register → Master
   ID → choose advertisement → tick every eligible inclusive group →
   pick exam centre → pay). Documents: citizenship (front & back, JPG),
   passport photo (JPG), signature (JPG), academic certificates (PDF),
   inclusive-category proof if applicable. Double-fee window: +7 days
   after deadline. Wrong formats / mismatched details are a common
   rejection reason.
   Source: https://wilson.com.np/how-to-fill-loksewa-online-application-form/

10. **Should I guess when unsure (negative marking)?**
    The repeated advice: gamble only if confused between *two* options;
    otherwise leave it blank — a blank loses nothing, a wrong answer
    costs 20%. For TSC the same rule is stated explicitly.
    Source: https://www.collegenp.com/service/teachers-service-commission-tsc-bhaktapur-tsc-exams-utility-service

11. **Is coaching necessary?**
    No — not mandatory. Self-study with the right materials, past papers
    and mocks succeeds for many candidates. Coaching is worth it mainly
    for structure, guidance and discipline.
    Source: https://www.kumarijob.com/blog/lok-sewa/how-to-pass-loksewa-exam

12. **What is the Constitution's most-asked content?**
    Promulgation 20 Sep 2015 (2072/06/03); 308 articles, 35 parts,
    9 schedules; 31 fundamental rights (Part 3, Arts. 16–46); federal
    democratic republic; 7 provinces; 753 local levels; HoR 275
    (165 FPTP + 110 PR), National Assembly 59; President 5 years,
    min. age 45; PM min. age 35; 33% women's representation in Parliament.
    Sources: https://lokshewa.online/blog/lok-sewa-gk-important-questions.html ·
    https://lokshewa.online/blog/nepal-constitution-important-questions.html

## 3. Prioritisation — what the question-bank expansion should build first

Based on search demand (§1) and on what the current bank already has.
Current bank (counted 2026-10-25): 1,129 questions — Constitution &
Governance 80, Economy & Development 55, Current Affairs 55, Science &
Tech 55, English 92, Computer & ICT 72, Nepali 60, IQ & Reasoning 110,
Maths & Numerical 110, Culture & Society 74, Nepal History 73, Nepal
Geography 73, Office Management & Public Service 60, Banking & Finance 60,
Education & Pedagogy 60, Health/Technical 40.

### Priority 1 — build first (highest demand, thinnest coverage relative to demand)

1. **Constitution 2072 — deep drill set.** Only 80 Qs in a combined
   "Constitution & Governance" topic, yet it is the single most-searched
   and most-tested topic. Add: article-number questions (esp. Arts. 16–46
   fundamental rights, directive principles Part 4), constitutional
   bodies (CIAA, PSC Arts. 286–288, Election Commission, Auditor
   General), federal/provincial/local powers, amendment history.
   Target: +60–80 Qs, split into a dedicated "Constitution 2072" sub-topic
   if the app supports it.
2. **Current Affairs — monthly rolling set (the only topic that goes stale).**
   55 Qs now, but demand is for *last 6–12 months* news, refreshed
   monthly. Add a dated monthly batch (budget, appointments, treaties,
   NRB monetary policy, international events) and label each batch by
   month; retire/flag batches older than 12 months.
3. **Repeated / old-question GK set ("Most-asked GK").** Students
   explicitly search for repeated questions from 2070–2082 BS papers.
   Build a tagged set drawn from documented past-paper repeats
   (Shey Phoksundo, Chitwan NP, Karnali, Rasuwa, Sati 1977 BS, etc.)
   with "asked in Kharidar/NaSu/Section Officer" labels where verifiable.
   This is the highest-ROI new *practice mode*, not just more questions.
4. **Kharidar Basic Office Skills (BOST) + Office Management depth.**
   Kharidar prelim is 20 of 50 Qs on BOST and the main Paper II is
   entirely Office Management — but the bank has only 60 office Qs
   shared across all exams. Add office procedure, record management,
   Civil Service Act 2049 / Regulations 2050, Local Government
   Operation Act 2074, Good Governance / RTI / procurement basics.

### Priority 2 — build next (strong demand, decent base to grow)

5. **IQ & Reasoning — timed / tricky set.** 110 Qs exist, but guides say
   this is the section students leave too late and lose on speed.
   Add number-series, coding-decoding, blood-relations, direction/ranking
   and non-verbal sets explicitly tagged by type, so the app can drill
   one type at a time.
6. **Maths — speed drills.** 110 Qs exist. Add percentage / profit-loss /
   ratio / SI–CI / time–work / time–speed–distance clusters with
   short-solution explanations (no calculator is allowed in the hall).
7. **Nepal History + Geography — high-yield facts.** 73 Qs each; expand
   the documented repeat-facts (unification, Lichchhavi/Malla, political
   movements 2007/2046/2062-63, rivers/lakes/parks, districts/provinces/
   headquarters) before adding obscure facts.
8. **Banking & Finance — NRB/RBB/NBL/ADBL set.** 60 Qs now; NRB prelim
   splits Management & Accounting 30 / Economics & Banking 30 / Maths 14 /
   Computer 14 / GK 12. Add BAFIA 2017, NRB Act, monetary-policy tools,
   banking-law basics, and unified-exam (RBB/NBL/ADBL) service questions.

### Priority 3 — build after (real demand, but narrower or later-stage)

9. **Governance / Public Administration (NaSu Paper II + Section Officer
   Papers II–III).** Main-stage material: contemporary issues, SDGs,
   fiscal federalism, public policy, HRM in civil service, e-governance.
   Valuable, but only after a candidate passes the prelim — build after
   P1–P2. Note: much of this is subjective in the real exam, so MCQ
   coverage should be framed as prelim/foundation, not main-exam prep.
10. **Education & Pedagogy (TSC) depth.** 60 Qs now. Add pedagogy,
    child-centred teaching, SESP / education acts, and subject-specific
    TSC sets (Maths, Nepali, Social Studies lower-secondary) — the
    subject papers are where TSC old-question searches concentrate.
11. **English + Nepali — main-exam writing support.** 92 + 60 Qs now.
    MCQ grammar/vocabulary is covered; essay/letter/précis cannot be
    MCQ-tested, so add at most a "writing prompts + model outlines"
    note rather than more MCQs.
12. **Health / Technical basics.** 40 Qs, thinnest topic — but demand is
    concentrated in Staff Nurse / HA / NEA / NTC tracks, not the core
    PSC audience. Expand only if a health/technical exam is added as a
    named target; do not grow it ahead of P1–P2.

### Explicitly deprioritise
- New *exam* targets (Police/Army/NEA/NTC, driving licence) before the
  Kharidar/NaSu/Section-Officer/TSC/NRB core is deep — they appear in
  searches, but as secondary tracks on other sites, not as the lead demand.
- More generic Science & Tech trivia (55 Qs) ahead of Constitution /
  Current Affairs / repeated-GK — it is in the syllabus, but no source
  ranks it among the most-searched topics.
